# Copy Review & Direction — English

A briefing for rewriting the English copy on ookopsignal.eu. The design has been overhauled to feel like a grassroots movement — bold, warm, tactile. The copy needs to catch up. Right now it reads like documentation for a tool. It should read like a friend pulling you into something good.

---

## The core problem with the current copy

The copy keeps explaining *what the tool is* rather than *what it does for you*. It's self-referential ("I have developed a tool that lets you add a badge"), technical ("end-to-end encryption"), and hedging ("seems simple, but in practice it can be a hassle"). The brand identity calls for a **friendly activist** — someone who's direct, warm, slightly cheeky. The current English copy is none of these things. It's polite, passive, and reads like it was written by someone describing their side project.

### Specific issues

1. **Developer voice, not movement voice.** "I have developed a tool" — who cares? The user doesn't need to know someone developed something. They need to know what they can do *right now*. Same energy as "this website uses cookies" — nobody reads it, nobody's moved by it.

2. **Over-explaining the problem.** The chicken-and-egg framing ("Why move if no one in your network uses Signal? And why use Signal if no one moves?") is a good insight buried in a wall of text. It should be a punchy one-liner, not a paragraph.

3. **Badge, not "tool that lets you add a badge."** The user is here to get a badge on their photo. Call it what it is. "Add an Also on Signal badge to your profile picture" — done. No need to describe the meta-concept of "a tool that does X."

4. **Passive constructions everywhere.** "The photo is processed only in your browser" — fine for a privacy note, but the overall copy leans heavily on this tone. It should feel active, energetic, forward.

6. **"How it works?" is grammatically wrong.** Should be "How it works" or "How does it work?"

7. **Benefit descriptions are flat.** "Signal is a foundation without shareholders" is a fact. "No shareholders, no ads, no catch" is a message. The benefits read like Wikipedia, not like reasons to care.

8. **The CTA is generic.** "Join us! Together we can make the move easier!" — this could be for any cause. It doesn't say anything specific about Signal, badges, or the actual action we want people to take.

## Tone targets

| Instead of... | Write like... |
|---|---|
| "I have developed a tool" | "Add the badge" |
| "This badge shows that you're also on Signal" | "Let people know you're on Signal" |
| "Signal is a privacy-focused messaging app that protects your communication with end-to-end encryption" | "Signal keeps your conversations private. Only you and the person you're talking to can read them. That's it." |
| "Why move if no one in your network uses Signal?" | "The hardest part about switching? Going first." |
| "Join us! Together we can make the move easier!" | "Be the reason someone switches." |

**Reference voices:** Oatly packaging copy (cheeky, self-aware, surprisingly warm). Signal's own website (clean, confident, no fluff). Patagonia (activist but not preachy).

## Section-by-section direction

### Hero (intro.title + intro.subtitle)

**Current:**
> Move to Signal — and help others do the same!

**Problem:** "Move to Signal" is a command with no hook. "Help others do the same" is vague.

**Direction:** Lead with the social proof angle — the badge is about showing you're there, which makes others follow. The subtitle should hint at *why this works*, not just repeat the action.

**Example range:**
- "Show them you're on Signal" / "One badge. A thousand conversations."
- "Be the reason someone switches" / "It starts with your profile picture."
- "Also on Signal?" / "Make it visible. Make it easy."

### Intro body (whySignal.intro1 + intro2)

**Current:**
> Moving from WhatsApp to Signal seems simple, but in practice it can be a hassle. Why move if no one in your network uses Signal? [...] I have developed a tool that lets you add an AlsoonSignal-badge to your profile picture.

**Problem:** Two dense paragraphs before the user knows what to do. The "I developed a tool" framing centres the maker, not the user. "AlsoonSignal-badge" is clunky as a compound noun.

**Direction:** One short paragraph max. Name the problem in one sentence, then immediately pivot to what the user can do about it. Don't explain the tool — just describe the action.

**Example direction:**
> The hardest part about switching to Signal? Going first. When nobody in your circle is on Signal, why would you switch? That's where the badge comes in. Add "Also on Signal" to your profile picture — and show your friends and family you've made the move. That's all it takes to get the ball rolling.

### "Read more" prompt (whySignal.readMoreQuestion + readMore)

**Current:**
> Do you want to know more on why to move to Signal? Read further

**Problem:** "Do you want to know more on why to move" is awkward phrasing. "Read further" is a literal Dutch translation.

**Direction:** Short, natural link text.

**Example:** "Why Signal? Here's the case." or just "Why Signal?" as the link.

### CTA (whySignal.callToAction)

**Current:**
> Join us! Together we can make the move easier! 🚀

**Problem:** Generic. Double exclamation marks. Feels like a startup landing page. The rocket emoji adds nothing.

**Direction:** One punchy line that makes the user feel like they're *doing something* by adding the badge. Less "join the club," more "start something."

**Example range:**
- "Be the reason someone switches."
- "Your profile picture is about to start a conversation."
- "Add the badge. Start the wave."

### How it works (howItWorks.step1–3)

**Current:**
> 1. Upload your profile picture
> 2. Add the badge
> 3. Use on WhatsApp

**Problem:** Step 3 limits to WhatsApp. The numbered prefix + emoji combo is redundant with the visual cards.

**Direction:** Drop the numbering from the text (the UI already shows progression). Make step 2 slightly more specific and step 3 platform-agnostic.

**Example:**
- Upload your photo
- Position the badge
- Use on WhatsApp

### Privacy note (note.privacy)

**Current:**
> The photo is processed only in your browser and is not stored anywhere.

**Direction:** This is fine. Maybe slightly warmer: "Your photo never leaves your device. We don't store anything." But honestly, functional copy here is good — trustworthy beats clever for privacy assurances.

### "Get Signal" section (getSignal.title)

**Current:**
> No Signal-app?

**Direction:** Slightly more inviting. "Don't have Signal yet?" or "Ready to get Signal?" — something that assumes they *want* it.

### "Find the tool" link on Why Signal page (whySignal.findTool)

**Current:**
> Go the tool

**Problem:** Typo ("Go the tool" instead of "Go to the tool"). But also: don't call it "the tool." Just tell them what they'll do.

**Direction:** "Get your badge" or "Add the badge to your photo"

### Nav items

**Current:**
> Tool | Why Signal?

**Problem:** "Tool" is meaningless as a nav label. What tool? A hammer?

**Direction:** "Get the badge" or "Add badge" or "Badge maker" — something that tells you what the page *does*.

### Why Signal page — body copy

The benefit titles are solid (Non-profit, Privacy by design, Ethical leadership, etc.) but the descriptions are dry. They read like they were written for a compliance document.

**Direction:** Keep the structure (title + short explanation) but make each one feel like a *reason to care*, not a fact to memorise. Lead with what it means for the user, not the organisational structure.

**Example rewrite of benefit 1:**
- **Current:** "Signal is a foundation without shareholders or venture capitalists and is funded by donations instead of ads."
- **Direction:** "No shareholders. No ads. Signal is funded by donations — which means they work for you, not advertisers."

**Example rewrite of benefit 2:**
- **Current:** "Signal collects no user data and offers the highest level of privacy. Even WhatsApp and Facebook Messenger use Signal's encryption protocol."
- **Direction:** "Signal collects nothing about you. Zero. Even WhatsApp uses Signal's encryption — they just don't share Signal's values."

### Download note (note.download)

**Current:**
> Note: If the download doesn't work in the LinkedIn app, open this page in a browser.

**Direction:** Fine as-is. Functional copy. Could be slightly more helpful: "Download not working? Try opening this page in your browser instead of the LinkedIn app."

## General principles for the rewrite

1. **Short sentences. Active voice. Second person.** "You" not "users." "Add" not "can be added."
2. **Don't describe the tool. Describe the action.** Nobody needs to know this is "a tool." They upload, they badge, they download.
3. **The problem is social, not technical.** The copy should emphasise the network effect ("be the first," "show your friends") over the privacy argument. Privacy is the *Why Signal* page's job. The main page is about *momentum*.
4. **WhatsApp is the foil — lean into it.** WhatsApp is the thing people are on, the thing they're switching *from*. Naming it directly makes the message concrete and relatable. Don't genericise it.
5. **Conversational, not casual.** Warm but not try-hard. No excess exclamation marks, no forced enthusiasm. Confidence is quiet.
6. **One idea per sentence.** The current copy stacks multiple concepts into long sentences. Break them up.

---

## What NOT to change

- The privacy note tone — functional and trustworthy is correct there.
- The benefit *structure* on the Why Signal page — titles + descriptions work well. Just sharpen the descriptions.
- The translation disclaimer — keep it factual.
- Button labels (Upload photo, Download, New photo) — these are clear and correct.
